    Workflows¶

    This page only covers dropping a JSON into ComfyUI. For story cards see usage, for per-beat tuning see motion library.

    The source files live in workflows/. The download links below are mirrored into docs/assets/workflows/ by scripts/sync_workflows.py at build time.

    Download¶

    Environment 3 beats 4 beats Notes
    UNet family (SDXL) workflow-sdxl.json workflow-sdxl-4beat.json CheckpointLoaderSimple, 20–30 steps, cfg 5–7
    DiT family (MOSS-Image) workflow-moss.json workflow-moss-4beat.json UNETLoader + CLIPLoader + VAELoader, 8 steps, cfg 1.0. Swap three loaders for FLUX / SD3 / Krea / Anima
    Preview (SDXL, 60f / 4 beats) — workflow-sdxl-preview.json SDXL sample: same seed, denoise 0.5, 30fps
    Preview (MOSS, 60f / 11 beats) — workflow-moss-preview.json Builds the repo preview: same seed, denoise 0.85, 20fps, 3.00 s

    24 frames is the default. For 8 / 30 / 60 frames you do not switch files:

    python scripts/emit_workflow.py --base workflows/workflow-moss.json \
    --story assets/story.diet.json --size 640 --out my-60.json

    --frames rebuilds the interpolate / decode / batch triplet; nothing else is rewired.

    Structure¶

    K keyframe KSamplers (shared first latent, shared seed)
  → N × LatentInterpolate
  → N × VAEDecode
  → ImageBatchMulti(N)
  → SaveAnimatedPNG + SaveAnimatedWEBP + VHS_VideoCombine(GIF)

    N is the frame count. Sampling happens K times (once per keyframe); interpolation and decoding are plain tensor ops, so doubling frames does not double compute.

    Knobs on the canvas¶

    Node What to change
    CLIPTextEncode "全局动作指导（所有关键帧共享）" Camera, lighting, framing, whole-clip motion. One edit applies to K0..Kn. Empty = bypass
    CLIPTextEncode "K0 首帧提示" / "K1 提示" / … What happens in this beat
    Every KSampler seed / steps / cfg / denoise / sampler / scheduler, per beat
    Note "可调参数清单" Cheat sheet written on the canvas
    Note "提示词写法（按架构分方言）" Three encoder dialects + Layout rules + frame table

    Beat length follows the LatentInterpolate distribution: more interpolation nodes in a segment means a slower beat. emit_workflow.py --segs lays this out for you.

    How to lay out transition frames¶

    Sixty frames are not sixty new images. Sampling still runs K times (once per keyframe); the frames in between are points on the latent line between two keyframes, decoded one by one. So "transition" has two layers:

    1. Inside a beat — each frame's ratio moves from the source keyframe toward the target keyframe. The segment's frame count is how many steps that move is cut into.
    2. Between beats — the last frame of a segment lands on the target keyframe; the next segment continues from there. A keyframe appears exactly once on the timeline.

    Where the seam stall came from¶

    The old curve ramped every segment from 1.0 to 0.0:

    seg0: K0 … K1(0.0)
seg1: K1(1.0) … K2(0.0)   ← K1 again

    The end of one segment and the start of the next were the same keyframe, i.e. the same image twice. Measured adjacent-frame difference at those seams was 0.06 — it reads as a freeze. The current layout:

    • segment 0 frame 0 is ratio=1.0 (the K0 cover), and its last frame is ratio=0.0, exactly K1;
    • every middle segment starts at 1/cnt and ends at ratio=0.0, exactly the next keyframe;
    • the last segment interpolates back to K0 but stops one step short, so looping into frame 0 does not hold the same image for an extra frame.

    Budget the frames per beat¶

    segs holds the frame count of each segment and must sum to frames. One rule: the bigger the pose change between the two ends of a beat, the more frames it gets.

    What the beat does Frames (out of 60)
    shoulders, head or mouth only 4–5
    raise a hand, turn, bow the head 5–6
    reach across the body, lift a large prop 7–8

    The eleven-beat Diet Betrayal uses 5,5,4,7,6,7,5,6,5,5,5: reaching for the phone (K3→K4) and lifting the chicken bucket (K5→K6) get 7 frames each; shoulder/head beats get 4–5; the final beat uses 5 frames to interpolate back to K0.

    To slow the whole film down, keep the frame count and drop fps on all three save nodes from 30 to 20 (3 s). The in-between count and per-frame step stay the same; only playback speed changes. Once the total length is set, use the story card's dwell to decide which beats hold longer — see Pace.

    python scripts/check_anim.py output/apng/anim60_00001_.png \
    --segs 5,5,4,7,6,7,5,6,5,5,5 --fps 20 --story assets/story.diet.json
    Reading Meaning Fix
    min < 0.5 duplicate frame, the seam stalls check that each segment does not ramp 1.0→0.0; check segs vs beats
    max > 8 one step is too large, reads as a cut give that beat 1–2 more frames before touching denoise
    drift > 20 colour drifts across the film check lighting words per keyframe and whether denoise is too high

    Import¶

    1. Open the matching ComfyUI instance.
    2. Drag the JSON onto the canvas.
    3. Point the loader at your local models:
    4. SDXL: CheckpointLoaderSimple
    5. DiT family: UNETLoader + CLIPLoader (use type=moss_image for MOSS-Image) + VAELoader
    6. Confirm the three save nodes use APNG/final/{apng,webp,gif}/....
    7. Press F5 before queueing, or the frontend may keep the old prefix.

    Environment differences¶

    UNet family (SDXL) DiT family (MOSS-Image)
    VRAM from 6 GB from 8 GB (512 / 640 verified)
    steps 20–30 8 (DMD distilled, do not raise)
    cfg 5–7 1.0
    keyframe denoise 0.35–0.45 0.82–0.90 (below 0.80 the keyframes barely move)
    text encoder CLIP (comma tags) Qwen3.5-9B (Chinese prose)
    prompt mode tags moss_zh

    Raising MOSS-Image steps to 20 or cfg to 6 only makes it muddier. SDXL is the opposite: below 20 steps or cfg below 5 it loses detail.

    MOSS-Image is one DiT model; SDXL is not DiT at all. Writing "Moss / DiT" side by side is a categorisation error: DiT is the architecture (arXiv:2212.09748, U-Net backbone replaced by a transformer), MOSS is a specific model. Swap in FLUX / SD3 / Krea / Anima and the denoise range is the DiT one (0.45–0.55), not 0.85. Per-model detail: Models.

    Prompt dialects¶

    The dialect follows the text encoder, not the model name. Three dialects cover every model in the table; full rules in Prompting.

    Encoder Models Mode One-line rule
    CLIP SD 1.5, SDXL, Illustrious, NoobAI tags comma tags, style last
    Qwen-VL family MOSS-Image, Krea 2, Qwen-Image, Anima moss_zh paragraphs, one full stop each
    T5 FLUX.1, SD3 / SD3.5 prose full sentences, no (word:1.3) 
    CLIP    : 1girl, solo, full body, … , <this beat>, anime screencap, clean lineart
Qwen-VL : 一间温馨的小卧室，深夜。 / 固定机位，平视，全身镜头…… / 小满：黑色短发……
T5      : A young woman with short black hair sits upright behind a low wooden table …

    Three hard rules:

    1. Change exactly one variable per beat. Change two and the interpolation gives you two people.
    2. Describe pixels, not feelings. shocked becomes eyes wide, pupils small, mouth open.
    3. Nail the framing. fixed camera, eye level, full-body, head to toe inside the frame, or the model crops to half body on its own.

    When text must appear in the frame (sign, poster, speech bubble), use absolute-pixel bboxes on a canvas whose sides are multiples of 16 — see Prompting.

    assets/story.diet.json is a complete eleven-beat moss_zh example (60 frames / 20 fps / 3 s); python scripts/make_story.py --preset diet-betrayal --out my.json regenerates it.

    Pace: fps and per-beat dwell¶

    Pace has three independent layers:

    Layer Decided by Effect
    Total length fps on the save nodes (story card fps) frames / fps. 60 frames @20 fps = 3 s
    Per-beat dwell story card dwell (one weight per beat) keyframes hold longer, transitions move faster; total unchanged
    In-beat easing story card ease (0–0.8, default 0.3) first and last frames of a beat get longer delays

    ComfyUI's save nodes accept one global fps only — per-frame dwell cannot be expressed there. So:

    # 1) render (all three save nodes share one fps)
python scripts/emit_workflow.py --base workflows/workflow-moss.json \
    --story assets/story.diet.json --size 640 --out diet60.json

# 2) rewrite per-frame delays after export: pixels untouched, no resampling
python scripts/retime.py output/apng/anim60_00001_.png --story assets/story.diet.json

# 3) web preview with the same timing
python scripts/make_preview.py output/apng/anim60_00001_.retimed.png \
    --story assets/story.diet.json --out-dir docs/assets --width 384

    Frames x frame rate¶

    Frames Slow (narrative) Standard Fast (chat)
    8 6 fps / 1.33 s 8 fps / 1.00 s 10 fps / 0.80 s
    24 8 fps / 3.00 s 10 fps / 2.40 s 12 fps / 2.00 s
    30 10 fps / 3.00 s 15 fps / 2.00 s 20 fps / 1.50 s
    60 20 fps / 3.00 s (default) 25 fps / 2.40 s 30 fps / 2.00 s

    GIF frame delay quantises to 10 ms (1 centisecond), so:

    fps Per frame Written to GIF Real 60-frame length
    8 125 ms 120 ms 7.2 s (2.9 s at 24 frames)
    10 100 ms 100 ms ✓ 6.0 s (2.4 s at 24 frames)
    20 50 ms 50 ms ✓ 3.00 s
    25 40 ms 40 ms ✓ 2.40 s
    24 41.7 ms 40 ms 2.40 s (not 2.5 s)
    30 33.3 ms 30 ms 1.80 s (not 2.0 s)

    Tick-marked rows divide evenly, so GIF will not silently change speed. APNG and WebP are millisecond precise and accept any fps.

    At least 0.20 s per beat¶

    Eleven beats over 60 frames at 20 fps averages 0.27 s per beat. A shoulder-only beat can take 4 frames (0.20 s); less than that is a flicker. retime.py and check_anim.py both flag beats under 200 ms.

    Diet Betrayal uses:

    0.95, 0.95, 1.1, 1.3, 1.0, 1.3, 0.95, 1.0, 0.9, 0.95, 0.95

    The phone reach (K3→K4) and the chicken bucket (K5→K6) get 1.3 — the two widest moves — while shoulder-and-mouth beats drop to 0.9.

    To slow down, drop fps first, then add dwell¶

    Same frame count, lower fps: the interpolation step per frame is unchanged, the whole clip just plays slower. That is the cheapest lever. Then use dwell for "these two beats should hold longer". Both are export-time only; neither resamples.

    Do not add frames to fix "too fast": more frames make the transition finer, not each beat longer.
